Safe and computationally efficient local planning for mobile robots in dense, unstructured human crowds remains a fundamental challenge. Moreover, ensuring that robot trajectories are similar to how a human moves will increase the acceptance of the robot in human environments. In this paper, we present our ICRA 2026 accepted work - Crowd-FM, a learning-based approach that decouples the generation of safe trajectories from the evaluation of their human-likeness. Our approach has two novel components. We first utilize a Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) model to rapidly generate a diverse batch of kinodynamically feasible, collision-free trajectories. Subsequently, we train a scoring function purely on a dataset of human teleoperation demonstrations in dense crowds to evaluate the environmental context and select the trajectory that best aligns with human expectations. We demonstrate that this two-stage approach successfully bridges the gap between expressive generative modeling and intuitive human-robot interaction.
